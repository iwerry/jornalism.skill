# Verification Knowledge

## Purpose
Verification of claims, documents, user-generated content, images, video and audio.

## Claim verification
1. Extract the smallest checkable proposition.
2. Identify speaker, date and context.
3. Locate the primary evidence.
4. Check the relevant time period and population.
5. Seek independent corroboration.
6. Document the verification path.
7. State unresolved elements.

A claim can be partly supported and still misleading because of omitted context.

## User-generated content
Treat social posts as leads until provenance and context are established.

Check:
- original uploader;
- first known publication;
- upload time versus event time;
- location;
- prior copies;
- edits/crops;
- surrounding posts;
- independent material from the same event.

## Image and video
Use a layered process:

`source → provenance → metadata → visual consistency → geolocation → chronolocation → archival comparison → independent corroboration`.

Useful checks include:
- landmarks and terrain;
- road signs and language;
- weather and shadows;
- architecture;
- public event schedules;
- satellite/street imagery where lawfully available;
- earlier copies of the same image.

A detector score is not authentication.

## Document verification
Inspect:
- issuer;
- numbering;
- dates;
- signatures/seals;
- typography and formatting;
- metadata;
- internal consistency;
- references to external records;
- confirmation with the issuer when feasible.

A scanned document is not automatically authentic because it looks official.

## Fact-check verdict discipline
Do not force a categorical verdict.

Use a verdict only when the evidence supports it. Otherwise state what is known, what is missing and why it cannot be established.

## Information disorder
Distinguish:
- false information shared without intent to deceive;
- deliberately deceptive information;
- harmful true information used out of context or to cause harm.

Do not infer intent merely from the falsity of a claim.
