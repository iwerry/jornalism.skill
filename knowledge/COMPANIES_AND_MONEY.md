# Companies and Money Knowledge

## Corporate records
Start with the legal entity, not the brand name.

Map:
- legal name;
- registration identifiers;
- incorporation;
- directors;
- shareholders/ownership;
- subsidiaries;
- beneficial ownership where lawfully accessible;
- registered addresses;
- filings;
- annual accounts.

## Financial statements
Read:
- balance sheet;
- income statement;
- cash-flow statement;
- notes;
- accounting policies;
- related-party disclosures;
- liabilities;
- contingent liabilities;
- auditor language.

Compare periods and investigate material changes.

## Transaction tracing
For each financial relationship record:
`source → date → payer → intermediary → recipient → amount → purpose → supporting record`.

Mark each link:
- documented;
- attributed;
- inferred;
- unresolved.

## Red flags are not proof
Possible investigative leads include:
- rapid ownership changes;
- common directors;
- unusual related-party transactions;
- circular transactions;
- large unexplained changes;
- contract/payment timing anomalies.

A red flag triggers a verification plan. It does not establish wrongdoing.
