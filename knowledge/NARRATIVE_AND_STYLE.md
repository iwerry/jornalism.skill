# Narrative and Style Knowledge

## Purpose and boundary
Craft techniques for making a verified story compelling to read — without ever letting craft substitute for, exaggerate, or outrun the evidence. This module sits underneath `knowledge/EDITORIAL.md` and `knowledge/INVESTIGATION.md` §5 (narrative construction) and does not override either.

**Persuasive** here means *reader-engaging*, not *argumentative*. This skill still separates fact, attributed claim, inference and opinion at all times (Golden Rule 2 / `SKILL.md` rule 5). A technique from persuasive or rhetorical writing is adopted only if it can be applied to a wholly evidenced sentence; if applying it would require softening a qualifier, omitting a contradiction, or implying certainty the reporting lacks, it is rejected — no exception for how much it improves the read.

## Structural techniques (safe to use on verified material)
- **Concrete opening scene** — start on a specific, verified moment or detail rather than an abstract summary; this is a sequencing choice, not a factual one.
- **Nut graph** — after the scene, state plainly what the story establishes and why it matters, in `knowledge/EDITORIAL.md`'s neutral-attribution register.
- **Stakes framing** — tell the reader early who is affected and how, using only consequences the reporting actually supports.
- **Controlled pacing** — alternate scene, explanation and evidence rather than dumping all documentation in one block; this aids comprehension of a genuinely complex chain of evidence, it does not add persuasive weight beyond the evidence.
- **Active voice and specific verbs** — clarity techniques, not spin; "the audit found X" beats "it was found that X," but neither may claim more than the audit says.
- **Callback and payoff** — resolving a question raised in the opening at the point the evidence resolves it, rather than before.

## Techniques that are off-limits regardless of engagement value
- loaded or connotative adjectives not supported by the evidence (`knowledge/EDITORIAL.md`'s headline rule extends to body copy);
- rhetorical questions that imply an answer the story does not establish;
- omitting a material qualifier or a subject's on-the-record denial to make the narrative cleaner;
- false balance (treating a fringe or unevidenced claim as equally weighted to a well-corroborated one) — and its opposite, collapsing a genuinely unresolved question into false certainty;
- borrowing the emotional register of advocacy or opinion writing in a piece labeled as reporting.

## Genre calibration
Apply narrative craft in proportion to the labeled genre from `SKILL.md` Phase F:
- **breaking news** — minimal craft, maximum front-loaded verified fact;
- **explanatory article** — clarity and structure techniques, light scene-setting;
- **investigative narrative** — full narrative toolkit, still bound by the rules above;
- **fact-check / data story / OSINT verification note** — plain, procedural register; narrative techniques are largely out of place here since the genre's value is its auditability.

## Style and voice localization
Tone, sentence length and formality should follow the target newsroom's own style manual (`knowledge/EDITORIAL.md` → style localization; `knowledge/INTERNATIONALIZATION.md` for jurisdiction-specific conventions such as the Brazilian annex). Craft technique choices in this file are additive to house style, never a replacement for it.
