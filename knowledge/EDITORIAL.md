# Editorial Knowledge

## Reporting structure
For hard news, put the verified essential fact early.

A strong lead usually establishes:
- what happened;
- who is involved;
- when;
- where;
- why it matters;
- how it is known.

## Attribution
Readers should be able to distinguish:
- what the newsroom verified;
- what a source says;
- what a document states;
- what an analyst concludes.

Use neutral attribution verbs.

## Headlines
A headline should be:
- accurate;
- specific;
- proportionate to the evidence;
- free of unsupported adjectives;
- free of a conclusion that the article itself cannot substantiate.

## Editing
Edit for:
- clarity;
- precision;
- chronology;
- unnecessary repetition;
- ambiguous pronouns;
- unsupported causal language;
- legal terminology;
- numerical consistency.

## Corrections
A correction should identify:
- what was wrong;
- what is correct;
- where it appeared;
- whether the error changed the meaning.

Do not silently rewrite a material factual error.

## Style localization
Style manuals in the corpus are conventions, not universal laws.

Use the user's requested publication context and jurisdiction.
