# Investigation Knowledge

## Purpose
Operational synthesis of the investigative-reporting, narrative and verification sources in the library.

## 1. Start with a hypothesis
A strong investigation begins with a question that could be proven wrong.

Use:
- observable proposition;
- expected evidence;
- competing explanation;
- falsification test;
- evidence deadline.

Do not write the conclusion into the hypothesis.

**Source families:** S06, S07, S08, S44, plus the verification sources S03–S05 and S48.

## 2. Follow the paper trail
For institutional or financial investigations, map the document sequence rather than collecting isolated documents.

Typical chain:
`policy/decision → tender/procurement → contract → amendments → invoices/payments → delivery → audit/oversight → litigation or sanctions`.

At every link record:
- document identity;
- issuing body;
- date;
- relevant field;
- authenticity status;
- relationship to the hypothesis.

## 3. Triangulate
Independent corroboration is stronger than repetition.

Two articles quoting the same leaked spreadsheet are one evidentiary lineage, not two independent sources.

Ask:
- same original source?
- same financial interest?
- same time/place?
- same document lineage?

## 4. Adversarial test
Before publication, deliberately search for:
- honest error;
- coincidence;
- incomplete context;
- changed policy;
- accounting convention;
- duplicate records;
- alternative actor;
- alternative timeline.

If the alternative explanation survives, revise the hypothesis.

## 5. Narrative construction
An investigation can be evidence-rich without being readable.

Build the narrative around:
- verified central finding;
- human or institutional consequence;
- documentary sequence;
- explanation of mechanism;
- contradiction or response;
- unresolved limitations.

The narrative must never outrun the evidence.

## 6. Right of reply
Convert every serious allegation into concrete questions.

Record:
- allegation/fact being put to the party;
- evidence category;
- questions;
- date sent;
- reasonable deadline;
- response;
- refusal/non-response;
- how the response changes the story, if at all.

## 7. Publication threshold
Use the internal evidence scale plus corroboration. A high-confidence source can still be wrong. A low-confidence source can generate a useful lead.

**Operational rule:** no central accusation should rest solely on an anonymous tip or unverified social post.
